# European Startup & Tech Intelligence Source Registry

## Purpose

Use this document as the base source registry for an application that
monitors meaningful startup, venture capital, AI, deeptech, software,
technology-business, acquisition, and ecosystem developments across
Europe.

The goal is **high coverage with low noise**, not maximum headline
volume. Local publications are important because significant stories
from a country's startup ecosystem may never reach international
outlets.

## Operating principles for the app

1.  **Use the three-layer model**

    -   **Layer 1 --- Pan-European:** establish a cross-border baseline.
    -   **Layer 2 --- Local:** discover stories first reported within
        individual markets.
    -   **Layer 3 --- Cross-border intelligence and primary sources:**
        discover patterns, verify material facts, and fill gaps.

2.  **Do not treat every source as equally authoritative.** A source's
    inclusion is a recommendation to monitor it, not a certification of
    every article.

3.  **Prefer original reporting.** Give greater weight to articles with
    named reporters, interviews, exclusive documents, independently
    sourced facts, original data, or clear on-the-ground reporting.

4.  **Separate reporting from announcements.** Company and investor
    press releases are useful for confirming that an announcement was
    made, but should not automatically be treated as independent
    validation.

5.  **Deduplicate stories.** Cluster articles about the same event.
    Prefer the earliest credible original report and retain other
    articles only when they add meaningful reporting or independent
    confirmation.

6.  **Track evidence and uncertainty.** Store the article URL,
    publisher, publication date, author when available, country, topic,
    primary/secondary status, and the evidence supporting important
    claims.

7.  **Use local-language sources.** Translate headlines and article text
    when necessary; do not exclude a source just because it is not
    English-language.

8.  **Do not infer that a country has no startup activity when its local
    coverage is sparse.** Use regional sources and primary announcements
    to reduce blind spots.

9.  **Revalidate this registry.** Check URLs, publication activity,
    ownership, editorial focus, language, and recent original reporting
    at least quarterly. Publications and their quality can change.

## Suggested source-quality labels

These labels are operational guidance for the application, not a claim
that every article from a source meets the standard.

-   **Core local newsroom:** A regular source to monitor for that
    market; still assess each article.
-   **Business/context newsroom:** Useful for major company, investment,
    market, and policy developments.
-   **Specialist/sector newsroom:** Useful for a particular area such as
    technology, digital business, or startups.
-   **Ecosystem/discovery source:** Useful for startup directories,
    events, community activity, and announcements; do not assume
    independent journalism.
-   **Primary source:** A company, investor, regulator, public
    institution, or research organisation speaking for itself. Use to
    confirm what that entity announced, not as independent assessment.
-   **Regional supplement:** Helps cover several countries or smaller
    markets but should not replace local reporting where it exists.

## Layer 3 --- Primary sources and cross-border verification

Use these to verify material facts and discover developments that media

### Company and investor sources

-   Company newsroom, blog, or official press page.
-   Investor and venture-capital firm announcements.
-   Official acquisition, merger, and funding announcements.
-   Public company filings and regulated market announcements where

-   Startup accelerator, incubator, and university technology-transfer

**Important:** A company or investor announcement is primary evidence
that the announcement was made. It is not independent proof of
product-market fit, customer outcomes, revenue quality, or the truth of
promotional claims.

## Recommended application workflow

For each candidate story:

1.  **Discover:** Find it through a local newsroom, pan-European source,
    specialist source, or primary announcement.
2.  **Resolve:** Identify the country, company, investors, sector, and
    event type.
3.  **Deduplicate:** Group reports about the same event and preserve the
    earliest credible source.
4.  **Assess provenance:** Label the story as original reporting,
    independently confirmed reporting, press release/announcement,
    ecosystem listing, or unverified claim.
5.  **Verify:** For consequential funding, acquisition, regulatory, or
    performance claims, seek a primary source or a second independent
    report.
6.  **Score relevance:** Prioritise material developments in funding,
    exits, company growth, technology breakthroughs, product launches
    with evidence, major customer wins, executive changes, policy, and
    notable failures.
7.  **Summarise without exaggeration:** Clearly distinguish confirmed
    facts, claims made by interested parties, and the app's
    interpretation.
8.  **Keep a coverage log:** Track which markets produce unique stories
    and which sources repeatedly add value.

## Suggested record fields for an implementation

When converting this Markdown into an application database, use one
record per source with fields such as:

-   `source_name`
-   `country`
-   `region`
-   `url`
-   `languages`
-   `source_role` --- `local_newsroom`, `business_context`,
    `specialist`, `ecosystem_discovery`, `primary_source`, or
    `regional_supplement`
-   `coverage_topics`
-   `priority` --- `core`, `secondary`, or `situational`
-   `original_reporting_confidence` --- initially `unverified`; update
    only after review of recent articles
-   `access_method` --- `rss`, `newsletter`, `website`, `api`, or
    `manual`, once verified
-   `last_checked_at`
-   `status` --- `active`, `review_needed`, or `inactive`
-   `notes`

Do not assume an RSS feed, API, unrestricted access, or permission to
crawl exists merely because a source has a website. Verify each site's
current access method and terms before implementation.

## Maintenance policy

-   Check source URLs and recent publishing activity quarterly.
-   Review the last 10--20 relevant articles before marking a source as
    a trusted original-reporting source.
-   Downgrade sources that mostly republish announcements or duplicate
    other outlets.
-   Add a new source only when it provides meaningful local coverage not
    already captured.
-   Keep an audit trail of changes to source status and quality
    assessments.

## Initial implementation recommendation

Start with **Layer 1 plus two or three high-value local sources in each
priority market**, then expand to the full registry. Begin with Sweden,
Germany, France, Finland, Denmark, the Netherlands, Spain, Italy,
Poland, Switzerland, and Austria. For smaller ecosystems, use one local
business or technology source plus a regional supplement.

The desired outcome is not to ingest every article. It is to identify
the **smallest credible set of sources that captures the highest share
of consequential European startup and technology developments**.
